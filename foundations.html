01 · CONCEPTS AND RELATIONSHIPS

A word gains meaning from its relationships

“Cold” and “hot” make sense by contrast along a shared semantic axis: thermal sensation. This does not mean that physical heat depends on words. It means that the conceptual meanings of cold and hot are relative to a common frame.

Cold ↔ Hot → Lukewarm → Temperature
Duality · a relationship

Two poles can be compared because they share a criterion. The line between them represents semantic coherence, not a measurable distance.

Equilibrium · a candidate

“Lukewarm” can be an intermediate judgement, relative to an observer or context. It is not a universal temperature threshold.

Synthesis · an integration

We look for an interpretation that connects both poles without collapsing into either. Such a synthesis requires additional justification.

Integrating category

“Temperature” is a measurable physical magnitude that helps place all three descriptive labels within a shared framework.

This is a conceptual model, not an automatic rule that every pair of words must form a meaningful opposition or generate a third category.

02 · QUANTITY, QUALITY AND PERSPECTIVE

What an observer measures, and what an observer notices

Quantitative descriptions assign values and coordinates; qualitative descriptions classify relationships, similarities, differences and change. Both can describe the same event: “the object moved 3 metres” is quantitative, while “the object approached us” describes a relationship.

Independent binary axes produce sets of 2, 4 or 8 combinations, represented by a segment, square or cube. The geometric dimension counts independent coordinates, rather than levels of awareness.

Snapshot A

At position x = 2, temperature T = 18 °C.

Snapshot B

At position x = 5, temperature T = 22 °C.

Comparing the snapshots gives Δx = 3 and ΔT = 4 °C. If A and B are ordered as observations, we can identify succession. A measured time interval additionally requires a clock or other temporal reference. Two photographs alone do not generate physical time or determine its direction.

The author's proposal to treat time as a quality of spatial change is a philosophical interpretation; it is not a result established by the cube, by PPR or by the quantum experiments.

03 · INTERACTIVE SEMANTIC DIFFRACTION

Explore how two conceptual poles balance

IA_m models concepts and relationships as a graph. Semantic Diffraction compares two source-centered Personalized PageRank fields to find a node structurally influenced by both sources. This simplified lab uses invented normalized influence values; it does not run PPR, retrieve a graph, or validate actual concepts.

Step 1 · Shared axis

Choose a pair with a meaningful criterion, such as cold versus hot along a thermal axis.

Step 2 · Independent propagation

Run one structural walk from cold and another from hot. For a candidate n, denote their resulting weights pA(n) and pB(n).

Step 3 · Joint influence and imbalance

Reward influence from both poles and penalize the absolute difference. λ adjusts this penalty.

Step 4 · Structural audit

Exclude unrelated axes, overly popular hubs or inappropriate roles, then test stability when perspective or constraints change.

S(n) = pA(n) + pB(n) − λ|pA(n) − pB(n)|

04 · A DISTINCT, EXACT MATHEMATICAL MODEL

How this connects to Cubo64

Cubo64 moves from conceptual diagrams to a finite, reproducible construction. It has 4³ = 64 positions and uses arithmetic over GF(4). The function labels each position with an odd number from 1 to 31, repeated four times across the cube.

A real 4 × 4 section

Coordinates x = 0; click any cell for its GF(4) calculation.

Exact properties

All twelve coordinate planes are 4 × 4 magic squares: rows, columns and their two main diagonals sum to 64.

72 of 76 examined lines sum to 64. The four space diagonals do not. Two inscribed regular tetrahedra share a centroid and each has vertex labels summing to 64.

The star of tetrahedra is a known geometrical figure. The distinctive observation here is its numeric relationship with the chosen GF(4) labeling.

Read the mathematical proof

u = x XOR (2·y) XOR (3·z)
v = x XOR (3·y) XOR (2·z)
G = 4u + v
F = 2G + 1

Here 2·y and 3·y refer to multiplication in GF(4), not ordinary integer multiplication. This cube is not derived from the semantic diffraction score. Both belong to a broader research interest in balance, relational structure and symmetry.

05 · RELATED RESEARCH

One line of inquiry, different kinds of evidence

IA_m

Builds conceptual graphs involving oppositions, equilibrium candidates and syntheses.

Semantic Diffraction

Audits candidate balances using structural propagation and axis constraints.

Structural Coherence

Examines how graph relationships behave under a change of conceptual perspective.

Q12 and Emergent Triad

Study constrained combinatorial states. A space of 12 binary variables has 4096 possible configurations; specific constraints select smaller sets.

The computational results do not demonstrate a physical theory of the universe or consciousness, nor do they identify these different mathematical models as the same object.
